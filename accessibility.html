Accessibility statement

Built to work for everyone

Accessibility is a big part of my work, so it should be part of this website too. Here's what I've done, what I'm still working on, and how to tell me if something doesn't work for you.

My goal

I built this site to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA. These are the international guidelines for making websites usable by people with disabilities. I wrote the site by hand in HTML and CSS, which gave me control over how every part of it works.

I'm a student, not an accessibility auditor, and the site hasn't had a formal audit. I'd rather be honest about that than claim it's perfect.

What I've done

Seeing the content

  • I checked every text colour against its background. All text meets the WCAG AA contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1. My brightest pink is only used for decoration and large display text, and I use a deeper pink for links and buttons.
  • Text is set in sizes that can be zoomed up to 200% without breaking the layout.
  • The layout adjusts to phones, tablets, and computers.
  • Information is never shown by colour alone. For example, the skill chips on my About page are written out in words.

Using a keyboard

  • Everything can be reached and used with a keyboard, including the flip card on my homepage and the filters on my Experience page.
  • A clear outline shows where you are as you move through the page.
  • A "Skip to main content" link appears when you first press Tab, so you don't have to tab through the menu on every page.

Using a screen reader

  • Pages use proper headings, landmarks, and lists, so they can be navigated by structure.
  • Meaningful images have text descriptions. Purely decorative drawings, like the footprint trail and the rotary phone, are hidden from screen readers so they don't add noise.
  • Changes on the page, like the number of roles shown after filtering or copying my email address, are announced.

Motion

  • Animations are small and only respond to scrolling or clicking. Nothing flashes.
  • If you've turned on "Reduce motion" in your device settings, the animations stop and everything is shown in its finished state.

What I'm still working on

This site is still growing, and so is this list:

  • Testing: I'm testing pages with a keyboard and with VoiceOver, the screen reader built into Mac and iPhone, as I add them.
  • Documents: my résumé and any project reports I share will be tagged, accessible PDFs.
  • Video: my introduction video will have captions and a written transcript when it's added.
  • Project images: charts and screenshots on my project pages will have descriptions of what they show, not just what they are.

If something on this site doesn't work for you, please tell me. I'll do my best to fix it, and I can send you the information in another format in the meantime.
